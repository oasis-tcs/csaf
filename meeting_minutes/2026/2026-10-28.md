### CSAF 2.1 CSD04 Public Review

- Thomas Schmidt reported that
  [CSAF 2.1 CSD04](https://docs.oasis-open.org/csaf/csaf/v2.1/csd04/csaf-v2.1-csd04.html) was in public review and that
  **three review comments** had been received as of this meeting. The
  [published review period](https://docs.oasis-open.org/csaf/csaf/v2.1/csd04/csaf-v2.1-csd04-public-review-metadata.html)
  runs from October 23 through November 6, 2026, ending at 23:59 UTC.
- One comment had not yet been transferred into the GitHub issue list. It concerned the converter's treatment of an
  advisory whose `initial_release_date` differs from the initial entry in its revision history.
- Thomas Schmidt explained that the CSAF 2.0 to CSAF 2.1 conversion rules could replace the initial release date to align
  it with the revision history. The newly introduced validation test would reject an inconsistent CSAF 2.1 document.
  Changing the initial release date could also move the document between distribution year directories; he gave an example
  of an initial date moving from 2021 to 2023.
- An alternative would be to preserve the initial release date and add an appropriate revision-history entry. Thomas Schmidt
  cautioned that changing the specified conversion behavior would constitute a material change and could affect the plan
  to proceed directly to CS01. He noted that the input documents under discussion were already inconsistent.
- Sonny van Lingen asked how an implementation problem could amount to material feedback on the specification.
  Thomas Schmidt clarified that the concern was the conversion behavior required by the specification, rather than merely
  a defect in one tool. Members were asked to consider the trade-off before the **November 9** meeting.
  **No resolution or change to the conversion rules was approved at this meeting.**

### GitHub Issues

#### Issue [#1719](https://github.com/oasis-tcs/csaf/issues/1719) — Can the JSON schemas get a copyright licence?

- **Background:** The issue concerns the permissions needed to modify and redistribute CSAF JSON schemas as part of
  packaged implementations. Its author reported that the current terms prevented inclusion of embedded schemas in a
  Debian package of the Go CSAF tools. The request was raised for **CSAF 2.0**, outside the CSAF 2.1 public review, although
  the same question could also affect the CSAF 2.1 artifacts.
- Thomas Schmidt explained that the restriction on modifying the standard created practical questions for implementations
  that adapt or package the schemas. Examples included encoding additional-property checks, restricting a producer to
  predefined document categories, and combining referenced CVSS or SSVC schemas into one resource.
- Sonny van Lingen supported a permissive approach but asked what the TC could decide under OASIS legal and IPR rules.
  Thomas Schmidt said that he had already raised the matter with the TC administrators and legal staff. A set of approved
  license options was not yet available, and one possible route was a Board-level resolution applying across TCs.
- Celia Savidge asked about the scope and enforceability of the copyright restrictions. Thomas Schmidt described potential
  interactions between copyright permissions, patent commitments and conformance that needed legal clarification.
- Sonny van Lingen asked whether permitting redistribution of unmodified schemas would suffice. Thomas Schmidt explained
  the distinction between changes that contradict CSAF and implementation-specific adaptations that still produce compliant
  documents. Stefan Hagen emphasized that conformance depends on implementation behavior and does not necessarily require
  direct use of the supplied schema, and supported seeking a practical solution from OASIS.
- **Motion:** Thomas Schmidt moved that the TC state its support for allowing JSON schemas to be packaged with
  implementations and request that the TC administrators and OASIS legal staff identify a solution to enable this.
  Sonny van Lingen seconded. No objections, further discussion or concerns were raised. **The motion carried by unanimous
  consent.** Thomas Schmidt undertook to communicate the TC's position to OASIS. The
  [outcome was recorded on the issue](https://github.com/oasis-tcs/csaf/issues/1719#issuecomment-6476557188).
- **No particular license was selected or adopted.** The motion expressed the TC's position and requested legal and
  administrative follow-up.
